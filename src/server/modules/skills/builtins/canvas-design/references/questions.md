# Ask before you build

Ask only when someone is there to answer. A manager puts its questions in the chat. A worker puts them where it puts every question for the user: as an open question on its plan. With nobody to ask (a delegated run, or "just make it"), decide, build, and say in one line what you assumed.

## When to ask, and how much

Read everything they gave you first. If two or more decisions that would change the result are still open, send one message with at most four questions before you write anything. If only one is open and it has a default you can defend, take the default and say so. A complete brief or a small revision gets no questions.

## What to ask

A good question could only come from someone who read the brief. Say what you read, name what it leaves unsettled, and put first the decision that would change the design most. Do not ask for what the conversation already answered. Where the situation implies an answer, take it and say that you did. The standard intake questions (who is it for, how long, screens or a prototype) are for a brief that is truly empty.

Give each question two to four answers to pick from, and make them real directions for this piece, such as "lead with the reorganisation" and not "a narrative approach". They differ along a line you can name; shades of one idea are not options. Put the one you would choose first and mark it (recommended). A question of fact gets no recommendation. Never offer "you decide".

## After the answers

The answers are decisions: repeat them in your one-line statement of assumptions. Anything they did not settle is yours to settle: do it, and tell them what you picked. A second round, again of no more than four questions and all of them new, is for two cases only: they want to be asked more, or one of their answers raised something that could not have been asked earlier. Never ask the same thing twice.

## Questions for a canvas

Ask about whatever their page, app or document does not settle for this design: which of several jobs the screen does first, who arrives on it, what of their product to keep and what to depart from, and what the artboards should differ in for the choice to be easy. If they name a product and attached nothing, ask for a link or a screenshot before designing blind. When there is no product to match and nothing suggests a look, put two or three directions into words, or draw two to four rough artboards and let those be the question. Whether they want screens or a prototype is read from the signals in SKILL.md. If there are none, choose and tell them which.

An example. They point you at their app's Billing page (a plan table, an "Upgrade" link in the page header, usage meters further down) and ask for "a clearer upgrade prompt for teams about to hit their limit". A message that fits:

1. Upgrade already sits in the header and the meters show usage. Where should the prompt appear? Beside the meter that is nearly full (recommended: it is where the limit becomes visible) / A banner across the page / A dialog on the next action that would exceed it.
2. The page is plain: tables and numbers, no illustration. How loud may the prompt be? In the page's own style, one accent colour (recommended) / One warmer moment with a short line of copy / A full comparison of the two plans.
3. Members and Storage have limits of their own. Should their prompts match this one? Billing only (recommended) / All three as a set.
